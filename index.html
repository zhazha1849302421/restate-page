TL;DR Learning-based multimodal CoT methods usually chain separately trained stages (generate a CoT, then answer). ReState instead trains one MLLM as a shared state-update operator: given an empty, correct, or erroneous reasoning state, it outputs a refined state and an answer. Trained also on its own historical states and applied recursively at inference, the same model constructs, preserves, and corrects its chain-of-thought — no critic, reflection module, or separate correction stage.

01

Stage-centric vs. state-centric

Most learning-based MCoT methods assign reasoning behaviors to predefined stages with stage-specific models, mappings, or objectives. ReState asks a different question: not which stage is the model in, but what reasoning state does it currently have, and how should that state be updated?

02

Method: URSM + CRSL

Two complementary components. URSM puts construction, preservation, and correction into one shared state space; CRSL closes the loop by training on the model's own historical states and recursing at inference.

Unified Reasoning State Modeling

URSM — one operator, three behaviors

(St+1, Ât+1) = Mθ(X, St),   St ∈ {φ, R+, R−}

X = {image, question, options, caption}; φ is the token [EMPTY_SOLUTION]. Each epoch, every sample yields two views: one with φ and one with a non-empty state, where R+ and R− are sampled with equal probability.

    04

    VersaCoT-10K

    10,000 single-image multiple-choice samples with visually grounded, quality-controlled CoT across eight heterogeneous visual reasoning domains — 1,250 per domain, split 7 : 1 : 2 into train / val / test.

    10,000samples
    8domains × 1,250
    170.9avg CoT words (median 162, range 50–537)
    2–9options per question
    1. 1Collect & standardize

      Reasoning-intensive samples from 8 public benchmarks, unified into one multiple-choice format with options, answer, and image caption.

    2. 2Annotate & filter

      Gemini-3-Flash writes visually grounded CoTs. Rule filters (200–4,000 chars, answer-leak phrases) plus a separate Gemini-3.5-Flash judge (3 binary checks + 5 scores; ≥ 8 kept).

    3. 3Augment states

      Following RAD, each CoT gets positive states (diverse, semantically consistent) and hard negative states (similar, semantically reversed). Historical states Rh are generated online during training.

    Number of options per question

    Option counts inherited from source tasks are kept, rather than forcing a fixed answer space.

    05

    Results

    All comparisons below use the same backbone and training setting. Across three backbones, ReState beats Direct SFT, Multimodal-CoT, and MIND by 0.90–4.60 pts on VersaCoT-10K, 0.83–2.87 on ScienceQA, and 0.18–13.07 on M³CoT.

    Best average on every dataset × backbone

    9 / 9 matched-backbone settings, with gains spread across domains and sub-tasks rather than one category — e.g. M³CoT Theory 61.90 → 90.48 over Direct SFT (Qwen3.5-9B).

    Not just more inference rounds

    Without iterative self-refinement — i.e. at inference cost comparable to MIND — ReState still leads MIND by 0.15–0.90 pts on VersaCoT-10K.

    Cheaper to train and serve

    Training time 9.86 h → 6.78 h (−31.2%) vs. MIND, and one model to deploy instead of two.

    Comparison with CoT frameworks

    Iterative self-refinement

    Round 0 is the initial reasoning. One extra round already adds 1.10–2.65 (VersaCoT-10K) and 1.99–3.62 (M³CoT) pts; performance then stabilizes. Reported numbers are the peak dataset-level accuracy along a fixed 9-round trajectory — no per-sample round selection.

    Break-down ablation

    SHS = training-time self-history states; ISR = inference-time iterative self-refinement (both part of CRSL). URSM alone already gives up to +4.96 pts (M³CoT).

    Every reasoning state matters

    VersaCoT-10K, removing one state type from full ReState. Dropping R⁺ or Rʰ costs up to 2.40 / 2.45 pts on Qwen3.5-9B.

    Better CoTs, not just better answers

    Judge-based CoT quality on VersaCoT-10K (Qwen3.8-27B as judge, 0–10). ReState scores highest on every metric for all three backbones.

    Cross-dataset generalization

    Trained on VersaCoT-10K only, no target fine-tuning. On M³CoT, Direct SFT falls below the original model on two backbones, while ReState improves all three. On ScienceQA, ReState is 0.52 below the original Qwen3-VL-8B but still above Direct SFT.

    07

    Limitations

    • Task format. Experiments focus on multiple-choice reasoning; open-ended VQA, video, and longer-horizon reasoning remain to be tested.
    • Inference cost. Recursive updates call the same model several times. Most gains arrive in the first few rounds, and without any iteration ReState still beats MIND. More efficient recurrence (e.g. looped Transformers) is future work.
